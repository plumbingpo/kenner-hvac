From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Kenner homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Kenner HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians watch the startup step by step and measure at each stage: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, so airflow issues are caught before they cut years off the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. The result is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
