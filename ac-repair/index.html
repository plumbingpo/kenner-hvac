Most AC breakdowns in Kenner trace back to a small part: the run capacitor. It helps start the compressor and condenser fan, loses strength slowly over the years, and tends to give out on the hottest afternoon, which is why so many service calls begin with an outdoor unit that hums while the fan sits motionless. Other frequent culprits include contactors with pitted, burned points, fan motors whose bearings have run dry, slow refrigerant leaks that steadily rob capacity, and evaporator coils that turn into blocks of ice once a dirty filter chokes the airflow.

At Kenner HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because a single warm-air symptom can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Central air, heat pumps running in cooling mode, and ductless mini splits from every major brand are all in our wheelhouse. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, most AC repairs are completed the same visit. When to Call for AC Repair in Kenner

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Frost or ice forming on the copper lines or the indoor coil
  • An AC that turns on and off every few minutes but never cools down the house
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • Water leaking around the indoor unit or from the ceiling below it
  • A condenser fan that sits still while the outdoor unit hums
  • Bubbling or hissing noises that point to a possible refrigerant leak
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • Higher summer bills even though the house still feels warm and humid

Why Hire a Professional for AC Repair?

Air conditioning repair concentrates several hazards in one cabinet. Capacitors store a charge that bites long after the breaker is off, condensing units run on 240-volt circuits, and the refrigerant loop is a sealed, pressurized system that federal rules do not permit venting to the air; working on it takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a can from the internet. The diagnostic traps are just as real: a frozen coil looks like a refrigerant problem but is just as often an airflow problem, and refilling a leaking system without repairing the leak wastes money while acid and moisture quietly damage the compressor.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Shade helps, but never enclose the outdoor unit; it needs free airflow on all sides
  • Have the AC serviced every spring, ahead of the first real heat
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
  • Avoid shutting registers in unused rooms, which raises static pressure and stresses the blower
