Size matters more than most people expect with a new furnace. An oversized unit short-cycles, overshoots the thermostat, and wears out early, while a correctly sized furnace runs longer and more quietly and costs less to operate. So Kenner HVAC starts every replacement in Kenner with a load calculation, not the number on the old nameplate. From there, the choices come into focus: 80 percent AFUE or a condensing furnace in the mid-90s, single-stage, two-stage, or modulating burners, and a basic PSC blower or a variable-speed ECM motor that saves electricity and smooths out temperature swings. The best fit depends on your home, your ducts, and how long your heating season lasts, and you will be living with it for the next twenty years.

The work that determines a furnace's lifespan happen out of sight. On a condensing furnace, the acidic condensate needs a proper trap, a correct slope, and a real drain, and the PVC venting must match the manufacturer's specifications for size, pitch, and termination, or the result ranges from repeated lockouts to flue gas problems. The gas connection gets a sediment trap and a leak test. Airflow gets measured too: we read static pressure across the system so the ductwork is not strangling the new equipment, and we correct restrictions while the system is open if the numbers show a problem. Where a permit is required, we take care of the process.

Commissioning is the step most installations skip and ours never do: we run combustion analysis to confirm the burners are tuned, set gas pressure to spec, measure temperature rise against the data plate and adjust the blower to match, verify the venting drafts, and run the furnace through full cycles while we watch. Then we walk you through the thermostat, the filter, and what the first season should look like. The Benefits of Professional Furnace Installation

What separates a good furnace installation from a bad one is mostly invisible once the job is done. The venting on a condensing unit has to match the manufacturer's tables for size, length, slope, and termination, or it will trip pressure faults and can put flue gases where they should never be. Gas lines need correct sizing, a sediment trap, and an instrument leak test. Condensate drains need real slope, because acidic condensate flows every day of the heating season and a flat line ends up as a midwinter lockout or a leak into the framing. Because none of this is visible in a finished photo, shortcuts tend to hide right here.

Sizing and commissioning are the other half of the story. A furnace picked by rule of thumb is usually oversized, and an oversized furnace lives a hard life of short cycles while delivering worse comfort. Our experienced technicians size from a load calculation, then commission with instruments: combustion analyzed, gas pressure set, temperature rise measured and the blower adjusted to hold it in the rated band, static pressure checked so the duct system and furnace are not fighting each other. That final hour of measurement is what turns a box of parts into twenty years of quiet heat, and it is standard on every Kenner HVAC installation.

Furnace Installation Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Clear leaves, drifted snow, and nests away from the intake and exhaust pipes
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on condensing models, especially before winter
  • Have the furnace inspected each autumn to keep burners tuned and readings on target
  • Use moderate thermostat setbacks; a correctly sized furnace prefers steady work to sprints
  • Start a regular filter schedule right away, since good airflow protects the new furnace
  • File the install paperwork and model numbers where you can find them for future service calls
  • Keep paints, solvents, and chlorine cleaners out of the furnace area
